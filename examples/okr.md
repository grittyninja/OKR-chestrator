# Information Security OKRs

## Overview
This document outlines the Objectives and Key Results (OKRs) for enhancing the organization's information security posture. The focus is on systematically improving the security maturity level, guided by industry best practices and relevant frameworks.

## OKR Summary Table
| Objective | Key Results | Initiatives | Tasks | Timeline | Owner |
|-----------|-------------|-------------|-------|----------|-------|
| O1: Enhance Overall Information Security Maturity | 3 | 11 | 62 | Q4 2025 | CISO |
| O2: Strengthen Infrastructure Security and Monitoring | 3 | 9 | 51 | Q4 2025 | Head of Infrastructure |
| O3: Minimize Attack Surface and Bolster Endpoint/Product Security | 3 | 10 | 59 | Q4 2025 | Head of Security Operations |

## Detailed OKRs
### Objective 1: Enhance Overall Information Security Maturity
**Description**: Increase the organization's overall Information Security Maturity score, using the agreed assessment framework (e.g., based on NIST CSF tiers), from the current baseline of Mid Tier 1 (Score: 17.7) to a target of Upper Tier 2 (Score: 45-50). This provides a quantifiable measure of progress across multiple security domains.
**Timeline**: Q4 2025
**Owner**: CISO
**Complexity**: High

**Measurement Criteria**: Achieve a final Information Security Maturity score between 45-50 based on the agreed assessment framework (e.g., NIST CSF). Measured via final assessment report.
**Timeline**: Q4 2025
**Owner**: CISO
**Complexity**: High

**Improving Information Security Maturity:**

Increasing information security maturity requires a strategic and systematic approach. Key frameworks like NIST CSF, ISO 27001, and CIS Controls provide structured guidance.

**Key Concepts & Frameworks:**

1.  **NIST Cybersecurity Framework (CSF):**
    *   Provides a risk-based approach with five core functions: Identify, Protect, Detect, Respond, Recover. (Source: SentinelOne, Cyberday.ai)
    *   Uses four tiers (Tier 1: Partial to Tier 4: Adaptive) to represent the rigor and sophistication of cybersecurity risk management practices, which can guide maturity improvement. (Source: Secureframe, Hyperproof)
    *   Primarily used by CISO/IT security teams but relevant to GRC and audit functions. (Source: Hyperproof)
2.  **ISO/IEC 27001:**
    *   An international standard for information security management systems (ISMS). (Source: SentinelOne, Cyberday.ai)
    *   Can be complemented by frameworks like O-ISM3 (which adds management controls and metrics) and CIS Controls (for technical hardening). (Source: Secureframe, Cyberday.ai)
3.  **CIS Controls:**
    *   Offer a prioritized set of technical security best practices developed by a community of experts. (Source: Cyberday.ai)
    *   Focuses on technical hardening and complements management-focused frameworks like ISO 27001 and NIST CSF. (Source: Cyberday.ai)

**Best Practices for Maturity Improvement:**

*   **Executive Buy-in:** Secure support from leadership and the board. (Source: Secureframe)
*   **Regular Assessments:** Conduct periodic cyber maturity assessments (using frameworks like NIST CSF) to monitor progress, identify gaps, and update plans based on the evolving threat landscape and business needs. (Source: SentinelOne, Verve Industrial)
*   **Develop a Roadmap:** Based on assessments, create a target maturity roadmap outlining initiatives across processes, technology, and training. Prioritize initiatives, potentially starting with 90-day "quick wins" covering baselining (e.g., asset inventory) and initial remediation (e.g., hardening). (Source: Verve Industrial)
*   **Continuous Monitoring:** Deploy tools and techniques for real-time threat detection and response. (Source: Secureframe)
*   **Risk Management:** Implement robust risk management practices. (Source: SentinelOne)
*   **Employee Training & Awareness:** Build a strong security culture through ongoing training and clear role definitions/accountability. (Source: SentinelOne)
*   **Maintain Compliance:** Ensure adherence to relevant regulations and standards. (Source: Secureframe)
*   **Technical Hardening:** Implement technical controls, potentially guided by CIS Controls, covering areas like software removal and baseline hardening. (Source: Verve Industrial, Cyberday.ai)
